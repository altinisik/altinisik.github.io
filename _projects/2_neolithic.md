The transition to farming in Southwest Asia was one of the most profound transformations in human history, and Anatolia and Upper Mesopotamia lay at its heart. During my postdoctoral years, I worked on the genomic history of the Neolithic in close collaboration with the [Computational and Evolutionary Genomics Group (CompEvo)](https://compevo.bio.metu.edu.tr/) at METU and the Human-G Laboratory at Hacettepe University.

My own work centred on **Upper Mesopotamia**. Studying 13 genomes from Pre-Pottery Neolithic Çayönü in the Tigris basin together with bioarchaeological and material culture data, we showed that this community was genetically diverse, carried ancestry from both the western and eastern Fertile Crescent and received immigrants, while also being organised along biological family lines. The study also documented practices such as head shaping and cauterisation, and identified Upper Mesopotamia as the likely source of eastern gene flow into Neolithic Anatolia {% cite Altinisik2022-pd %}.

Beyond Upper Mesopotamia, I contributed to studies of Neolithisation in other regions, including kinship and household organisation in Central Anatolia {% cite Yaka2021-jq Yuncu2025-zg %}, the expansion of farming into the Aegean {% cite Koptekin2025-zk %} and mobility across Holocene Southwest Asia {% cite Koptekin2023-xn %}, as well as to the development of computational methods for low-coverage ancient genomes {% cite Ceballos2021-zm Akturk2024-un Koptekin2025-cv %}.
