### Overview

How were families and communities organised in Roman Anatolia, and how did kinship, mobility and social structure shape one another? Ancient DNA makes it possible to address these questions directly from the people themselves, but doing so reliably requires well-preserved genomes and kinship methods that remain accurate on degraded, low-coverage data. This project builds that foundation, as preparatory work for a larger ERC Starting Grant research programme on kinship, mobility and social organisation in Roman Anatolia.

### Objectives

**Assessing DNA preservation.** We screen around 50 newly selected Roman-period skeletal remains from different regions of Anatolia, to identify the individuals best suited for high-coverage sequencing and to guide future sampling strategies.

**Deepening existing genomes.** We deep-sequence previously prepared libraries from 22 individuals that gave promising initial results. Higher-resolution genomes allow more reliable inference of both genetic relatedness and ancestry.

**Benchmarking kinship inference.** Using simulated ancient-like genomes embedded in known pedigrees, we test how accurately current methods classify relatives, and how they can be adapted to the low coverage and post-mortem damage typical of ancient DNA. The aim is a reproducible, validated framework for kinship inference in archaeological contexts. This work builds on our earlier benchmarking of kinship estimation tools and the development of DeepKin {% cite Akturk2024-un Guler2025-bc %}, as well as our studies of genetic and social kinship in Neolithic Anatolia {% cite Yaka2021-jq Yuncu2025-zg %}.

### Methods and open science

All genomic data are produced in clean-room laboratories dedicated to ancient DNA, using double-stranded library preparation and strict contamination controls, and analysed with established bioinformatic pipelines. Data will be shared publicly following publication, in line with open data principles. The project supports a doctoral fellow and a postdoctoral researcher.
