Human remains and archaeological sediments preserve DNA not only from people, but also from the microbes that lived with them and the environments they inhabited. Ancient pathogen genomes can reveal how diseases spread and evolved, while sedimentary ancient DNA (sedaDNA) can recover traces of past ecosystems even where no skeletal remains survive. I am interested in bringing these approaches to Anatolian archaeology, where they remain almost unexplored.

### Projects

**Leprosy in Anatolia: A Palaeopathological and Archaeogenomic Study**<br> Advisor · TÜBİTAK 1001 · 2023–2026<br> Combines palaeopathology with ancient DNA to study leprosy in Anatolia. As part of this work, Arda Sevkar's MSc thesis (2023), which I co-supervised, analysed *Mycobacterium leprae* in leprosy-affected individuals from Medieval to Late Ottoman Anatolia.

### Collaborations

I contributed to **aMeta**, an accurate and memory-efficient workflow for profiling microbial DNA in ancient samples that reduces false discoveries in ancient metagenomic analyses {% cite Pochon2023-ak %}. I also co-authored a perspective on integrating sedaDNA into archaeological research, outlining its potential for studying the origins, lifestyles and environments of past populations and the challenges that remain {% cite Ozdogan2024-wm %}.
